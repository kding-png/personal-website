Lumino

Lumino is a mushroom-shaped nightlight and companion app concept for children ages 1–5 and their caregivers. The system combines a pulsing light, audio playback, and an intercom to support bedtime routines and communication between children and caregivers.

Context

Methods of Human Factors Engineering
Team
Ginny Dong & Kylinn Ding
Scope
Research, app design, physical concept, and prototype testing

01 / The challenge

Supporting children and caregivers during bedtime routines

At bedtime, children may need comfort just as parents are trying to step away. Our task analysis mapped both sides of this routine: a child reaching for reassurance, and a caregiver repeatedly deciding whether to return to the room.

The design question became: how might we give children a simple, tangible source of comfort while helping caregivers stay responsive from another room?

02 / Research

Caregiver research and bedtime task analysis

We combined a CLIMBER analysis of the family’s context, a caregiver questionnaire with nine responses, and a task analysis of bedtime routines. Most children represented in this small sample were ages 3–4.

Caregiver involvement at bedtime

Caregivers described storytelling, singing, and talking as ways to help children settle. Most reported returning to the room one or two times during bedtime.

Accessible physical controls for young children

A young child in a dark room needs a physical action that does not depend on reading, navigating a screen, or precise finger movements.

Clear device status and caregiver controls

The parent-facing experience needs to make controls and device state easy to understand during an already demanding routine.

03 / The concept

Core functions: lighting, audio playback, and intercom

The concept integrates three functions: a pulsing light that provides a visual breathing cue, audio playback for preloaded stories or caregiver recordings, and an intercom for communication between the child and caregiver. The physical device provides direct access to these functions, while the companion app supports caregiver configuration. Effects on sleep outcomes have not been evaluated.

Physical form and tactile controls

The proposed silicone cap supports an open-palm pat instead of pinching or twisting. Distinct tactile controls, rounded edges, and a stable base informed the physical design. Material suitability, charging safety, and real-world use would still need validation before a manufactured product.

04 / The companion app

Companion app for device settings and caregiver communication

The app organizes the experience into Home, Story, Intercom, and Profile. Large controls, a warm palette, and consistent navigation support quick adjustments: changing brightness or volume, choosing audio, talking to a child, and reviewing usage history.

05 / Testing & iteration

Usability findings and interface revisions

We tested the app prototype with one parent of a four-year-old. The participant completed the core tasks, but the session exposed three points of friction. The findings guided a redesign; the report does not document a follow-up test of those changes.

  1. Home screen: scroll visibility

    Content below the visible area was initially missed. The revised Home screen partially reveals additional content to suggest that the page continues.

  2. Story screen: playback and selection controls

    The participant hesitated when finding playback. We moved the preview control beside “Add,” keeping listening and selecting within the same interaction area.

  3. Profile screen: connected-device information

    Uncertainty about the active device reduced confidence. The redesign added associated-device information to the Profile screen.

06 / Reflection

Project outcomes, limitations, and next steps

The project translated caregiver research and bedtime task analysis into a physical product concept and companion app. The usability session identified issues with content visibility, control grouping, and connected-device information, which informed the interface revisions.

The outcome is a researched product concept, physical design exploration, and an iterated app prototype. Next steps would include testing with a broader group of families, evaluating the redesigned screens, and validating the integration between the device and app. The nine survey responses and single usability session offer direction, not evidence of general effectiveness.
